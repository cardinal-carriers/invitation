The visual direction was chosen in Phase 0 and the opening animation was settled after it. This page is what happens when that direction has to survive a form, a table of 148 rows and a destructive confirmation — and the one rule that keeps it whole.

The rule

Everything in this product is one of two things, and the two never mix. If you can only remember one thing from this page, remember this one.

Paper is content

A real artifact: the invitation, the envelope, a card preview, the reply card. Textured, warm, tilted where a hand would tilt it.

  • Fraunces for display, Caveat for what a person wrote
  • Grain, fibre and fleck; wax; deckled edges
  • Guest-facing, or a preview of something guest-facing

Desk is chrome

The workbench the host operates: nav, tables, forms, dialogs. Same palette and same typefaces, but flat and quiet.

  • No tilt. No texture. No handwriting, ever
  • Karla, dense and legible at 148 rows
  • Gets out of the way of the thing being made

The two are literal CSS classes — .paper and .desk — so the rule is something you apply, not something you remember.

Colour

Warm neutrals carry the page; the accents are the four things on a real envelope — wax, postage, a sprig of green, and the paper lining. Nothing here is a brand colour looking for a job.

The perforation

Every division in this product is dashed, because a dashed rule is a tear line and everything here is something that could be torn off — a reply card, a receipt, a stub. It is the one structural motif, and it does all the dividing.

--perf, on section edges, table headers and card footers.

Buttons

One primary per screen, in wax. It sits on a small hard shadow, the way a seal sits proud of an envelope, and presses down when clicked.

Forms

Labels are always visible and always above. Help text explains a consequence, not a format. A field that asks for something private says what it is for.

Guests never see this.

The postal track

A letter has four states and this product genuinely knows all four — including opened, because the envelope animation firing is an event we record. So the track carries information, not decoration. It is the same four words everywhere: written, posted, opened, replied.

Words

The product speaks postal, not SaaS. This isn't decoration: "opened" is more precise than "viewed", "can't come" is kinder and shorter than "declined", and a host who thinks in letters makes better decisions than one thinking in campaigns. Use the left column everywhere — in the interface, in emails, in support replies.
